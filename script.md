# Script: System Overview (EcoLink Flow)

“This infographic shows how EcoLink works at a high level.

👉 First, universities browse and select educational trips that align with their curriculum and learning objectives.

👉 Then, EcoLink acts as the core engine.
We design the program, manage logistics, ensure safety, and prepare a full Faculty Approval Pack to simplify decision-making.

👉 Next, community-based ecotourism sites, or CBET communities, host the experience.
They provide guided tours, homestays, and cultural activities that reflect real local life.

👉 Finally, students engage in structured field learning.
They don’t just visit—they conduct research, create projects, and interact directly with real-world challenges.
**Flow Script Matrix**
Use one story throughout: “A faculty member is preparing a biodiversity field trip for 35 students and needs learning fit, logistics, safety, and approval documents.”

| Step | Website Movement | What You Say | Business Point |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Start on homepage | “EcoLink connects universities with community-based ecotourism sites, but the real product is not just discovery. It is the planning and approval workflow behind safe academic field trips.” | Position EcoLink as a coordination platform |
| 2 | Go to catalog / map view | “The journey starts with discovery. Faculty can explore available field sites geographically, which is important because distance, region, and access affect whether a trip is realistic.” | Discovery begins with operational context |
| 3 | Switch to photo view | “For comparison, the same destinations can be viewed as structured cards. These are not tourism cards; they show academic and planning information like location, activity, price per student, and duration.” | Destinations become field-site options |
| 4 | Use filters briefly | “Faculty can narrow the shortlist by region, activity, group size, season, and logistics readiness. This helps them find trips that fit their syllabus and constraints.” | Learning fit plus logistics fit |
| 5 | Open one package detail | “Now we move from browsing to evaluation. This page turns a destination into a field-trip proposal candidate.” | Detail page is the evaluation layer |
| 6 | Show overview hero and briefing | “The overview explains what students will experience, why the site matters, and what kind of learning outcomes it supports.” | Academic integration |
| 7 | Point to logistics snapshot | “Beside the learning value, EcoLink exposes practical information: duration, meeting point, capacity, transport pattern, activity level, and meals.” | Logistics transparency |
| 8 | Point to safety/trust/readiness info | “Safety is built into the planning surface, not hidden later. Faculty can see activity level, facilities, lead time, and readiness signals before submitting anything.” | Safety readiness |
| 9 | Use request sidebar | “At this point, the faculty member prepares a request. They add date, group size, course purpose, transport preference, and accessibility notes.” | Request preparation |
| 10 | Fill sample request | “For this demo, Professor Lina is requesting a trip for 35 students for a biodiversity module. EcoLink calculates the estimated total from the per-student pricing.” | Pricing and request context |
| 11 | Click review readiness / request | “Before submission, EcoLink checks whether the request has the essential information. This reduces incomplete requests and back-and-forth communication.” | Quality control before approval |
| 12 | Confirm request | “Once confirmed, the request becomes a workflow record. It is no longer just a page interaction; it becomes something faculty and admin can track.” | Request becomes workflow |
| 13 | Open planner / request timeline | “The planner is the shared workspace. It keeps the brief, timeline, documents, notes, and current status together.” | Single source of truth |
| 14 | Show documents / approval pack | “This is where EcoLink supports institutional approval. The trip brief, itinerary, and approval pack can be prepared from the same structured request data.” | Faculty approval pack |
| 15 | Switch to admin side | “Now we move to the admin view. Admins can review incoming requests, check readiness, verify documents, and coordinate operations.” | Admin review layer |
| 16 | Open pending request / trip | “The admin does not need to reconstruct the trip from messages. The learning purpose, logistics, group size, safety notes, and package details are already attached.” | Less coordination overhead |
| 17 | Approve request / trip | “After review, the admin approves the request. At this point, the trip moves from proposed to approved and can proceed into operations.” | Approval checkpoint |
| 18 | Show approved status | “This final status is the value of the system: everyone knows what was requested, what was reviewed, what was approved, and what documents support it.” | Closed approval loop |
